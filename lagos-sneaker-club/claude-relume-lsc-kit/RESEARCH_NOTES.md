# Research Notes

## Relume Workflow Notes

- Relume positions Site Builder as a flow from AI sitemap to wireframes to style guide, then export to Figma, Webflow, or React. Use Relume for structure and component selection first, then refine animation and exact polish after export.
- Relume says the sitemap and wireframes are linked: section titles and section descriptions inform the AI-generated wireframe section. For this project, write strong section names and descriptions before generating wireframes.
- Relume's sitemap prompting guidance recommends describing the company, goals, and important notes. Keep the prompt concise inside Relume, then use Claude for deeper strategy and iteration.
- Relume Design View applies the style guide across pages and is useful for getting most of the way to a finished design, but exact spacing, custom animation, and pixel-level refinements should be done after export.

Sources:
- https://www.relume.io/
- https://www.relume.io/ai-site-builder
- https://resources.relume.io/resources/docs/building-a-sitemap-with-ai
- https://www.relume.io/resources/docs/how-to-create-and-edit-wireframes-in-the-relume-site-builder
- https://www.relume.io/resources/docs/how-to-style-all-pages-using-the-design-view

## Motion / Emil Kowalski Notes

- Emil Kowalski's public `animations.dev` positioning is about web animations that feel right, with attention to easing, timing, spring motion, and practical implementation.
- Treat "Emil Kowalski style" as restrained design-engineering polish: interactions should be calm, intentional, responsive, and meaningful, not loud decorative motion.
- Ask Claude to use the Emil Kowalski / Animations on the Web Claude skill only if the user has it installed. Otherwise, use public motion principles: natural easing, short transitions, scroll-linked reveals with restraint, hover previews, reduced-motion support, and performance-safe transforms/opacity.

Sources:
- https://animations.dev/
- https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-an-animation-course
